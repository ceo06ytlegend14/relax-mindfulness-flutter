# Sanctuary: a practical path toward recurring revenue

Working plan — 2026-11-19. The owner asked for help turning the app into a sustainable business and confirmed **10+ hours per week**. This plan organizes that work; it does not promise income or authorize spending, publishing, signing or billing activation.

## The decision in one minute

**Start with one audience:** remote workers who already use background audio while working.

**Test one promise:** “Start your preferred focus atmosphere in seconds, without searching for another playlist.” This is a positioning hypothesis, not a clinically proven focus/productivity claim.

**Follow one sequence:** 10 useful customer conversations → a safe, owner-approved beta → 10 testers who return → the first 5 genuine paying customers → observe renewals and costs → consider measured growth.

**Spend $0 on advertising for now.** No new redesigns, speculative AI features, large sound counts as a marketing claim, or acquisition campaign before repeat use is demonstrated. Required store/content/testing expenses need separate owner approval; $0 advertising is not a claim that launching costs nothing.

This narrows marketing, not the product: preserve **Home / Sounds / Meditate / Sleep**, the approved midnight design, existing recipes, saves and access. Sleep and Meditate are not being removed. If interviews point to a stronger use case, review the evidence before changing this recommendation.

### Before an invited beta or paid release

Use the existing [launch gate](LAUNCH_HARDENING.md), [readiness ledger](LAUNCH_READINESS.md) and [device worksheet](release/DEVICE_QA.md); this plan does not replace them.

1. **Prove the current native candidate:** fresh analysis/tests/builds, signed device playback, background/lock-screen/interruption/routes/timer/fade checks, and actual listening. The independent HTML preview is not a native release.
2. **Resolve distribution rights and content quality:** review or replace unclear audio/artwork; keep source receipts, accurate labels and loop/listening evidence. Temporary-use permission is not a redistribution grant. Choose an explicitly cleared beta scope with the owner; do not imply the full library is ready.
3. **Preserve the 54-choice public-release target** unless the owner explicitly changes it. Early interviews need no completed library. A smaller cleared beta is a validation step, not silent completion of that requirement. Long masters and generated build artifacts stay out of Git; review bundle/download size, delivery, data usage and cost before bulk import or new hosting.
4. **Confirm real value, accounts, privacy and support:** verify existing app/customer identities, required credential-incident actions, support/legal destinations and data disclosures. Do not change identifiers or discard old customers.
5. **Before money moves:** approve delivered paid benefits and commercial access policy; test purchase, cancellation, pending, restore, expiry/refund and reinstall with real store sandbox products. Existing access/entitlements must be honored. Do not put pause, volume, basic timer or existing free recipes behind a new lock without a separate approved policy. Prices/periods come from actual localized offerings, never a hardcoded checkout promise.

Customer interviews about current habits can begin before a distributable build exists. Until sharing is approved, use words and research questions—not public uploads of the current audio, reference-derived art or unapproved builds. Do not accept deposits for undelivered recordings or represent a waitlist as payment validation.

## What monthly income would require

**Illustration only—not an approved price, forecast, observed conversion rate or statement of actual store fees.** Suppose a monthly customer price were **US$4.99**, with no annual plans/trials/discounts. Actual storefront prices, taxes, fees, refunds, currency conversion and costs must be taken from the chosen store and owner records.

| Active monthly subscribers at that hypothetical price | Gross monthly customer billings |
| ---: | ---: |
| 10 | $49.90 |
| 100 | $499.00 |
| 300 | $1,497.00 |
| 600 | $2,994.00 |

Those billings are **not take-home income**. For a deliberately simplified second scenario, assume combined deductions of **30% of billings** and **$50/month cash operating costs**. These are invented planning inputs, not verified costs. Cash surplus before income tax and owner labor = `paying subscribers × 4.99 × 0.70 − 50`.

| Desired monthly cash surplus in that scenario | Minimum whole paying subscribers | Scenario result before income tax and owner labor |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $500 | 158 | $501.89 |
| $1,500 | 444 | $1,500.89 |
| $3,000 | 874 | $3,002.88 |

These counts are arithmetic requirements under the assumptions, **not a plan that delivers those customers**. Reaching five real paying customers and learning why they return is a better first decision than buying traffic toward an untested $3,000 target. A hypothetical 10% monthly subscriber loss would require ten replacement customers per month just to hold 100 steady; that 10% is a scenario, not measured churn.

Do not treat annual upfront receipts as monthly recurring revenue, gross billings as profit, or an active trial as a payer. No paid pricing experiment should run until its delivered value, consent/disclosures and store configuration are approved.

## Weekly scorecard and decision rules

Copy the [blank weekly scorecard](business/weekly-scorecard-template.csv) into **owner-controlled private storage before entering sensitive business data**. The repository contains a template only, not actual revenue or user records. All actual fields start blank: unknown does not mean zero. Do not store names, contact details, health information, device identifiers or payment receipts in it.

- **Qualified interview:** completed conversation with a person in the proposed audience about a recent real work/background-audio experience. A reply, like, impression or hypothetical compliment is not an interview.
- **Problem confirmed:** a qualified participant describes both a recent recurring problem and their current workaround. Keep reasons in anonymous private notes; an opinion is not proof of payment intent.
- **Core task / activation:** independently choose/start an available mix, adjust a layer, minimize/reopen and pause successfully, then complete a real ten-minute listening attempt. Record task success and actual listening, not a selected timer, intended duration or automatically generated completion record. A failed attempt remains in the denominator.
- **Seven-day repeat use:** unique testers who use it on at least three different days within their first seven days. Divide only by enrolled testers whose full seven-day window has elapsed. Report each tester's outcome once, in the week that window completes, using that same cohort for both numerator and denominator—not this week's enrollments versus last week's returns. Do not divide by only the people who answered a follow-up or only successful activations. Unconfirmed use stays unknown; do not selectively count it as success.
- **Paying customers:** distinct real customers with a verified, non-refunded purchase; report new payers separately from all active paid customers. A restore is not a new purchase. A cancellation may still retain paid access until expiry; do not remove it early. Trials/test purchases are excluded.
- **Renewals:** use the same completed-period cohort for renewals due and successful real renewals; include cancellations that were due to expire instead of counting only attempted payments. Do not claim renewal evidence before a full paid period has elapsed. Keep monthly and annual plan periods distinct.
- **Cash result:** use platform-reported net proceeds for the period and subtract actual cash operating costs. Do not subtract fees/refunds twice if net proceeds already exclude them. This is a cash proxy before income tax and owner labor, not accounting profit or MRR; distinguish statement periods from payout timing.

Early usage can be recorded with consent through private tester diaries and observation; the app currently has **no newly implemented business analytics pipeline**. Any later analytics/attribution needs an explicit event definition, privacy review, minimal collection and verified instrumentation. Do not repurpose wellbeing/mood history as a marketing dataset.

**When results are weak:** identify the biggest loss between invitation → first use → repeat use → payment → renewal. Fix one problem, then retest a small cohort. If demand is weak after ten credible conversations, review the audience/promise; do not buy traffic or manufacture engagement. If activation is weak, fix the core experience. If repeat use is weak, investigate value/audio/reliability before the paywall. If people return but will not pay, test the delivered benefit and offer rather than applying pressure.

**Weekly review prompt:**

> Here are this week's anonymous counts, the exact current build, the three most common complaints and my available time. Choose the single highest-value next task, explain the evidence, implement or prepare it, and define what would make us continue or stop.

No guaranteed income, fake reviews/testimonials, automated spam, medical-effect promises, fabricated sales or implied business partnership. Persistence is useful when paired with evidence and a sustainable schedule.
