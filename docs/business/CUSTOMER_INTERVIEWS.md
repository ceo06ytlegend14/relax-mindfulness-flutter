# First ten customer conversations

Working kit — 2026-11-19. Supports the [revenue roadmap](../REVENUE_ROADMAP.md). These scripts are prepared, **not sent**. No participants, interviews, consent, sales or usage results are claimed.

## Who to invite

Start with remote workers who used background audio while working recently. Ask about their actual behavior, not whether they admire the app. Include people outside the owner's friends/family; do not recruit only enthusiastic supporters. Do not screen out people who say existing free alternatives already work well.

Use existing personal contacts or communities that explicitly allow research invitations. Follow their rules. No scraped contact lists, bulk unsolicited messages, fake identities, review exchanges or guaranteed-results promises. Ask permission before any follow-up. You need no public app release to ask about current habits.

## Invitation—copy and personalize

> Hi! I'm researching how remote workers use background audio during work. Would you be open to a 10–15-minute conversation about what you currently use and what gets in the way? This is research, not a sales pitch; no purchase or signup is required. It's completely fine to decline. I would take brief notes only with your permission.

Before starting, explain how notes will be used and obtain permission. Do not record audio, collect health information or store personal contact details in the repository. Use an anonymous participant code in private notes. Do not imply that consent to research is consent to marketing or analytics.

## Ask about the last real occasion

1. **“Tell me about the last time you used background sound while working. What were you doing?”** Ask when it happened and how long they listened. Avoid suggesting a problem they did not mention.
2. **“What did you use, and how did you choose what to play?”** Ask them to describe the actual sequence. How often did this happen in the past week?
3. **“What, if anything, was frustrating? What did you do about it?”** Ask for a recent example. “Nothing” is useful evidence; do not argue with it.
4. **“Have you tried another solution or paid for one? What made you keep it or stop?”** Participation is voluntary; no bank details, private receipts or proof-of-income request. Past spending is context, not a purchase of this app.
5. **“What would need to be different for you to change your current routine?”** Note this as an opinion or hypothesis, separate from observed behavior.

Only after the problem conversation, describe the concept neutrally:

> I'm exploring a sound mixer where you start a preferred atmosphere quickly and adjust individual layers. I don't yet know whether that solves the problem you described. What seems useful, unnecessary or worse than what you already use?

Do not lead with a feature tour or ask only “Do you like my app?” Do not claim clinically proven concentration, sleep treatment, narration, 54 finished recordings or a live checkout. Until distribution/media permissions are resolved and sharing is approved, do not send current builds or publish the current audio/artwork as a demo.

An interview confirms a problem only when the participant describes a recent recurring problem and their current workaround. Mark disagreement and lack of demand honestly. Ten positive reactions alone do not establish demand, retention or willingness to pay.

## Beta invitation—only after gates and owner approval

> We're running a small private test of a cleared, device-tested version of the sound mixer. Would you like to try it during your usual work and share what happens over one week? Participation is optional. We'll explain exactly what is collected before you choose. There is no obligation to buy or leave a review.

Only say “cleared” and “device-tested” after the evidence exists for the exact candidate. Use the approved distribution channel and actual privacy/consent terms; this text is not permission to distribute a build. Explain any real beta terms or incentives honestly. Never reward a positive review.

Observe a core-task attempt without coaching: choose/start a usable mix, change one layer, minimize/reopen, pause, and attempt a real ten-minute listening session. Record actual playback and barriers, not an intended timer duration. A first-task success is not repeat use. For the seven-day follow-up, ask on which days it was actually used and why it was or was not chosen; do not invent missing diary entries or send daily reminders that bias the return-use test.

## After ten conversations: make one decision

1. Group the problems people actually reported; separate audio quality/repetition, setup friction, reliability, value and other needs.
2. Count participants, not mentions. Record contradictory evidence and current alternatives that already solve the job.
3. Use the roadmap's small-sample rules only to choose another small experiment—not to claim market fit or authorize ads.
4. Choose one task and acceptance test. If the proposed audience has no meaningful unmet need, revisit positioning instead of adding more features to persuade them.

Do not charge, collect a deposit or send a paywall as a demand test before the real benefit, disclosures, store sandbox checks and sales/release approval are complete. A polite “I would pay” is not revenue.
